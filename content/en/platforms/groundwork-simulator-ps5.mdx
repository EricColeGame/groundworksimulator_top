## Groundwork Simulator PS5 vs. PC: What a Port Would Have to Solve

The single biggest technical obstacle isn't graphics — it's the simulation itself. The developer explicitly warns that detailed soil and water physics place a heavy load on the CPU, and that the game is demanding "even if its visuals may suggest otherwise." That's an unusual admission, and it's exactly the kind of workload that makes console ports tricky.

| Feature | How it works today | Console implication |
|---|---|---|
| Terrain deformation | Every bucket permanently reshapes the ground | Needs constant CPU simulation, not GPU tricks |
| Hydraulic feedback | Simulated resistance as the bucket loads | Suits adaptive triggers well |
| Water system | Pumps and hoses route water across the site | Requires precise placement controls |
| Vehicle transport | Load machinery onto trailers and haul it | Long, slow inputs favor analog sticks |
| Online co-op | Split jobs with friends across the site | Would need PlayStation Plus and crossplay decisions |
| Story mode | Guided tutorial teaching core mechanics | Straightforward to port |

The control scheme is the friendlier half of the problem. Excavator arms, bucket angles, and trailer alignment all map naturally to dual analog sticks, and the hydraulic resistance system is a genuinely good fit for adaptive triggers. The CPU cost is the harder half.
