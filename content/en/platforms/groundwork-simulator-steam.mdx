## What You Actually Do in Groundwork Simulator

The core loop is simple to describe and much harder to execute well: reshape terrain, find gold-rich ground, and build a washing operation that turns raw material into recoverable gold.

| System | What it asks of you |
|---|---|
| Excavation | Cut trenches, slice into hillsides, and pile material while managing bucket angle and hydraulic power |
| Terrain persistence | Every scoop permanently changes the site, creating new slopes, routes, and problems |
| Prospecting | Drill and test ground to locate deposits before committing equipment |
| Processing | Run material through sluice boxes, then finish concentrate on a shaking table |
| Water supply | Position pumps and route hoses so the wash plant stays fed as the site expands |
| Transport | Load machinery onto a trailer and haul it across rough ground to the next job |
| Traction and grades | Mud, inclines, and heavy loads force route decisions — sometimes you fix the road before you dig |

The most consequential design choice is the persistent terrain. Because the ground you leave behind becomes the foundation for the next task, sloppy digging compounds. A trench that saves five minutes now can block your haul route an hour later. That single mechanic is what separates Groundwork Simulator Steam from arcade-style digging games: you are managing a site, not clearing a level.

The second differentiator is hydraulic feedback. The excavator resists as the bucket loads up, so you adjust angle and power rather than holding a button. It sounds like a small detail, and it is the difference between operating a machine and watching one.

## Practical Tips for New Operators

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Starting a new site | Dig a wide access ramp first; narrow trenches become traps once you add a trailer |
| Loading the bucket | Ease off hydraulic power as material builds — full power on a full bucket stalls the lift |
| Prospecting | Test several holes before committing the wash plant; moving it later is slow work |
| Water setup | Route hoses along high ground so gravity helps and you are not re-plumbing every hour |
| Hauling | Reduce load or improve the grade before attempting a muddy climb repeatedly |
| Co-op | Assign fixed roles for a session; swapping machines mid-flow creates collisions and delays |
| Performance | Cap frame rate and close background apps — simulation ticks compete with everything else on the CPU |

Player experience with Early Access sims generally rewards patience: expect balance shifts, save-format changes, and the occasional rough edge. Treat your first site as a test bench rather than a perfect operation.
