## Why Groundwork Simulator PC Is a Different Kind of Digging Game

Heavy machinery simulators live or die on one question: does the dirt actually feel like dirt? Groundwork Simulator PC answers with a soil and water simulation engine that reacts to every bucket you move, and it arrived in Steam Early Access on October 22, 2026. That timing matters, because the launch price, the hardware demands, and the co-op options all shape whether Groundwork Simulator PC is worth installing on day one.

This is not a corridor-based trucking game or a clicker about ore. It is a worksite sandbox where trenches you cut become the roads you drive tomorrow, and where the ground you leave behind is the foundation for your next job. You run an excavation and gold recovery operation: prospect the hillside, dig promising material, wash it, and process the concentrate.

The catch is that all of that simulation costs something. Groundwork Simulator on PC leans hard on your processor rather than your graphics card, which changes how you should plan a build or a laptop purchase. Below you will find the full spec breakdown, the Early Access roadmap as the developers describe it, the core gameplay loop, and practical tips for co-op and performance.

## What You Actually Do During a Groundwork Simulator PC Session

The loop is straightforward to explain and surprisingly deep to play. You dig, you haul, you test, and you wash. What makes it engaging is that the terrain is persistent, so poor planning compounds — a badly placed ramp costs you time on every trip afterward.

| System | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Excavation engine | Simulated hydraulic resistance on every scoop | Bucket angle and available power decide how much material you lift |
| Terrain deformation | Each scoop permanently alters the ground | Your trenches, slopes, and ramps become tomorrow's infrastructure |
| Prospecting | Drill and test ground for gold-rich deposits | Stops you from washing barren dirt for an hour |
| Wash plant | Sluice boxes feed a shaking table | Turns raw material into recoverable concentrate |
| Water system | Pumps and hoses supply the washing operation | A dry plant produces nothing, no matter how good your digging is |
| Transport | Trailer and truck haulage between sites | Moving machinery is a skill of its own on tight turns and slopes |
| Online co-op | Shared worksite with divided roles | One player digs while another keeps material moving |

The realistic physics deserve a note. Hydraulic feedback means the machine resists you when the bucket bites into compacted ground, and the load in the bucket changes how the arm behaves. You are not pressing a button to remove a cube of terrain; you are managing angle, power, and weight.

Gold recovery adds a second layer. Instead of digging anywhere, you explore for rich ground, drill test holes, and only then commit equipment to a site. That pacing keeps the early hours from turning into pure earthmoving.

## Co-Op, Performance, and Buying Advice for Groundwork Simulator PC

### Split the Work in Online Co-Op

Online co-op is the feature that changes the pace of a session most. Solo, you alternate between digging and hauling, which means a lot of seat-switching. With a friend, the worksite becomes a production line.

- **Assign roles early.** One player on the excavator, one on transport, and swap only at natural break points.
- **Build access before you dig deep.** Cut a ramp to the pit while the ground is still easy to shape.
- **Test before you commit.** Drill holes and confirm the deposit before you drag the wash plant across the map.
- **Keep water within hose range.** Route the plant near a water source, or plan the pump chain before the first bucket.
- **Respect the load on slopes.** A trailer full of machinery on wet ground is how sessions end in recovery missions.

### Hardware Priorities That Actually Matter

Because the simulation is CPU-bound, upgrades should follow a different order than in a typical gaming build. The store page does not publish a full graphics menu, so treat these as general priorities rather than specific settings advice.

| Upgrade | Priority | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Memory: 16 GB to 32 GB | High | The recommended spec already asks for 24 GB, leaving little headroom |
| CPU: i5-8400 class to i5-12400 class | High | Soil and water physics run on the processor |
| SSD installation | Medium | Keeps a 10 GB install plus saves and page file responsive |
| GPU: GTX 1070 to RTX 3060 class | Medium | Helps visuals, but will not fix a simulation bottleneck |
| Handheld play | Low confidence | Steam Deck compatibility is listed as unknown |

Practical habits help too. Close browser tabs before a long session, since background memory pressure hurts a game that wants 24 GB, and cap your frame rate if you notice stutter tied to large terrain edits rather than to camera movement.

## FAQ

### Is Groundwork Simulator PC available on Steam Deck?

The store page currently lists Steam Deck compatibility as unknown, so there is no confirmed rating yet. Given how heavily the game leans on CPU-bound soil and water simulation, handheld performance is a genuine question mark. Check player experience in the Steam community before assuming it will run well.

### How much RAM does Groundwork Simulator PC need?

The minimum is 16 GB and the recommended is 24 GB on both Windows and macOS. That is high relative to the 10 GB install size, and the reason is the simulation itself rather than the visuals. If you multitask while playing, 32 GB gives useful breathing room.

### Does Groundwork Simulator PC support online co-op?

Yes. Online co-op lets you divide the work, with one player running the excavator while another handles hauling between the digging area and the wash plant. The store page notes in-game chat and online interactivity as part of the experience.

### How long will Groundwork Simulator PC stay in Early Access?

The developers expect roughly 3 to 6 months, though they note the timeline may shift with development progress and community feedback. The Early Access build already includes most planned features, one map, a range of vehicles, and a guided story mode, with additional maps and machinery planned along the way.
