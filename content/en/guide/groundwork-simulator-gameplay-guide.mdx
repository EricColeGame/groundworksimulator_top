## Your First Hour: A Step-by-Step Opening Plan

The fastest way to bounce off Groundwork Simulator is to grab the biggest machine and start carving. The fastest way to enjoy it is to treat the first session like a real site setup.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Play the story mode tutorial first | It walks you through the core mechanics in a guided sequence rather than dropping you into an empty pit |
| 2 | Master one machine at a time | Hydraulic feedback and bucket behavior differ enough that switching constantly teaches you nothing |
| 3 | Scout the site on foot before digging | You need to know where the slopes, mud, and water access are before you commit equipment |
| 4 | Drill and test for deposits | Test holes tell you where gold-rich ground is, so you do not wash hundreds of buckets of barren dirt |
| 5 | Reserve flat ground for the wash plant | A level pad saves you hours of re-leveling later |
| 6 | Solve water before you run material | A wash plant without a supply is just expensive scenery |

Treat that list as a checklist rather than a suggestion. Each item removes a failure mode that would otherwise cost you an hour of recovery work later in the session.

## Digging Technique: Making Every Bucket Count

The excavation engine simulates resistance, so the bucket does not simply swallow dirt. Angle, hydraulic power, and material buildup all interact, and the difference between a clean cut and a wasted scoop comes down to how you approach the face.

- **Set your angle before you drive in.** A shallow approach skims; a steep approach digs. Match the angle to whether you are cutting a trench floor, trimming a slope, or loading out.
- **Work in layers, not lunges.** Repeated controlled passes leave a cleaner floor and keep your machine stable.
- **Watch the load build.** As material fills the bucket, resistance rises — lifting too early spills the scoop back where you found it.
- **Plan the ground you leave behind.** Slopes you create become tomorrow's access roads or tomorrow's obstacle. Terraces and ramps are an investment.
- **Cut haul routes early.** A wide, gently graded path between the dig face and the wash plant pays for itself within a few dozen loads.

### Terrain Shaping Is a Tool, Not a Side Effect

Every scoop changes the landscape, which means site design is part of the job. Build up piles where you want a loading pad. Cut into a hillside to create a sheltered, level working area. Shape ramps at a grade your loaded truck can actually climb. If you only ever dig and never grade, you will eventually trap your own equipment in a pit with no exit.

## From Dirt to Gold: Building a Recovery Chain

Gold does not appear because you dug. It appears because you drilled, dug the right material, washed it, and processed the concentrate. The chain has four links, and a weak link wastes everything upstream of it.

| Stage | What you do | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect | Drill and test the ground for deposits | Identify gold-rich material before committing machines |
| Excavate | Dig the promising ground and load it out | Feed the plant with material that is actually worth washing |
| Wash | Run material through the sluice boxes | Strip away light material and capture concentrate |
| Process | Run concentrate across the shaking table | Separate the heavy values into a recoverable result |

Two habits separate efficient operations from chaotic ones. First, keep the plant fed at a steady rate instead of dumping a mountain of dirt and walking away — a backlog is just unprocessed material. Second, process concentrate regularly rather than letting it stack up, so you always know whether your current dig site is actually paying.

### Water First, Always

Water reaches your operation through pumps and hoses, and routing is a genuine design problem. Position equipment so hose runs are short, keep lines out of machine traffic paths, and add capacity as the site grows rather than rebuilding the entire network when throughput stalls.

| Element | Placement approach | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Pump | Near a reliable water source on stable ground | Parking it where a loaded truck will drive over the line |
| Hose runs | Short, direct, and routed around haul routes | Long snaking runs that snag on equipment |
| Wash plant intake | Positioned for easy servicing | Burying connections under stockpiled material |
| Expansion | Plan spare capacity from day one | Rebuilding the whole system when demand rises |

## Transport, Traction, and Fighting the Terrain

Moving machinery is its own minigame. Loading heavy equipment onto a trailer and hauling it by truck means lining up for the load, planning a route, and negotiating tight turns, slopes, and rough ground with significant weight behind you.

Mud, gradients, and loose ground turn every route into a decision. Watch your traction, choose your approach, and respect the load. Sometimes the correct next job is not digging at all — it is improving the ground under your wheels so you can get back to work.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Practical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wheels spinning on a grade | Slope too steep for the load | Cut a gentler ramp or dump part of the load and return |
| Machine bogging down | Soft, saturated ground | Lay material to build a firmer running surface |
| Trailer swinging wide | Tight turn taken too fast with weight behind | Approach wider, slow down, and straighten before the apex |
| Bucket refusing to fill | Poor entry angle or insufficient hydraulic power | Reset the angle and take a shallower, slower pass |
| Site feeling cramped | No planned staging area | Designate a flat pad for loading and plant access early |

## Co-op: Splitting the Work Without Stepping on Each Other

Online co-op is where Groundwork Simulator becomes a genuinely different game. Splitting the job by machine rather than by territory keeps traffic flowing and prevents two players from fighting over the same dig face.

| Role | Machine focus | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Excavator operator | Excavator | Cut the face, load haulers, and shape the site |
| Transport driver | Truck and trailer | Keep material moving between dig area and wash plant |
| Plant operator | Wash plant and water system | Maintain feed rate, water supply, and concentrate processing |
| Site hand | Any spare machine | Build ramps, fix drainage, and clear blocked routes |

Agree on a staging point and a one-way haul loop before the first bucket moves. Most co-op chaos in construction sims comes from two drivers meeting nose-to-nose on a narrow ramp, not from a lack of machines.

## Common Mistakes That Slow New Operators Down

- **Digging before prospecting.** Volume without assay is just landscaping.
- **Ignoring the tutorial.** The story mode exists specifically to introduce core mechanics through guided play.
- **Building the plant on uneven ground.** Everything downstream gets harder.
- **Neglecting haul routes.** A bad road taxes every single load.
- **Running the plant dry.** Water supply should scale with throughput, not lag behind it.
- **Never grading.** Self-inflicted terrain problems compound quickly on a one-map build.

Community reports from Early Access sims also tend to flag one more: refusing to restart a badly planned site. On a single map with permanent terrain deformation, a clean restart is often faster than unpicking a poorly designed pit.

## FAQ

**Is Groundwork Simulator multiplayer?**
Yes. You can run the operation solo or share the site in online co-op, splitting work between an excavator, transport, and the wash plant. As this Groundwork Simulator gameplay guide notes, agreeing on haul routes first prevents most co-op friction.

**How long will the game stay in Early Access?**
The developers expect roughly three to six months, though they note that timeframe may shift with development progress and community feedback. More maps and vehicles are planned.

**Do I have to play the story mode?**
You do not have to, but you should. It is a guided tutorial that introduces the core mechanics, and it is the quickest route to understanding excavation, water, and gold recovery.

**Is the game demanding on hardware?**
More than it looks. Soil and water physics are CPU-heavy, with 16 GB of RAM and a modern six-core processor listed as the minimum. Check the system requirements table above before buying.

**Should I buy now or wait?**
Early Access means an incomplete build with one map. If you want a polished, content-complete mining sim, wait; if you enjoy shaping a game's direction and reporting issues through the Steam Community hub and the official Discord, the current build already has the full core loop in place.
