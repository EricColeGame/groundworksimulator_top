If you have been digging hard and still feel broke, you are not alone — the game's realistic excavation, water, and hauling systems punish brute force and reward planning. ## Start With Test Holes, Not With a Full Bucket

New players usually pick the most obvious hillside and start stripping it. Experienced operators do the opposite: they drill and test first, then dig. Gold-rich ground is not evenly distributed, so a few cheap test holes can save you hours of hauling barren overburden to a wash plant that was never going to pay for itself.

The logic is simple. Your wash plant only earns money when pay dirt is running through it, and every load of low-grade material dilutes the concentrate, eats water, and burns time. Sampling tells you where the grade justifies the diesel.

| Step | What you do | What you are watching for | Why it protects your profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Drill | Sink a test hole on promising ground | Depth and quality of the layer | Tells you whether to keep going |
| 2. Sample | Pull material and check it | Signs of visible gold in the dirt | Confirms the spot is worth washing |
| 3. Mark | Flag the boundary of the rich zone | How far the deposit runs | Stops you digging past the pay zone |
| 4. Strip | Remove overburden separately | Clean separation from pay dirt | Keeps waste out of the sluice boxes |
| 5. Re-test | Sample again as you go deeper | Grade changes with depth | Prevents chasing a layer that has ended |

A practical rule from player experience: never strip a large area on a single positive sample. Dig a small confirmation pit first, then expand. Terrain changes are permanent, so the ground you destroy while testing is ground you cannot un-destroy later.

## The Groundwork Simulator Money Loop, Step by Step

Every profitable site runs the same loop: sample, strip, dig, haul, wash, concentrate, and repeat. Where most operations break down is at the handoffs — the excavator outruns the truck, the truck outruns the wash plant, or the wash plant sits dry because nobody ran a hose.

| Stage | Equipment involved | Output | Where the money is won |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sample | Drill and test tools | A decision | Avoiding wasted hauling |
| Strip | Excavator | Clean overburden piles | Faster access to pay dirt |
| Dig | Excavator with realistic hydraulics | Buckets of pay dirt | Full buckets, not half scoops |
| Haul | Truck and trailer setup | Material at the plant | Short, flat, repeatable routes |
| Wash | Pumps, hoses, sluice boxes | Washed material | Steady water, steady feed |
| Concentrate | Shaking table | Recoverable gold | Clean, consistent processing |
| Reset | Dozer-style site prep | Better ground | Cheaper next cycle |

### The Seven Habits That Pay

1. **Sample before you strip.** Two test holes cost minutes; a wasted stockpile costs hours.
2. **Keep the plant close to the dig.** Every extra hundred feet of haul distance is paid on every single load.
3. **Feed the plant evenly.** A sluice box that is starved, then flooded, loses fine gold that a steady feed would have caught.
4. **Route water before you dig.** Pumps and hoses laid out in advance mean the plant never idles waiting on a connection.
5. **Stockpile by grade.** Keeping rich dirt and marginal dirt in separate piles lets you run the good material when time is short.
6. **Fix traction before you fix output.** Mud and steep grades quietly tax every trip; a graded ramp pays for itself fast.
7. **Load the trailer properly.** A machine that slides off a trailer ramp costs far more time than the extra minute spent lining up.

## Wash Plant Setup: Where Most Gold (and Money) Gets Lost

The washing stage is the heart of the operation. Material goes through the sluice boxes, and the concentrate is finished on the shaking table. If either step is starved, clogged, or badly positioned, you are paying to move dirt that never converts into recovery.

| Component | Job | Common money leak | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pump | Moves water to the plant | Plant running dry mid-load | Place the pump where it stays primed |
| Hoses | Carry water across the site | Kinks and long detours | Short, direct runs you can see |
| Sluice boxes | Capture gold from washed material | Uneven feed and poor angle | Level the unit and feed at a steady rate |
| Shaking table | Finishes the concentrate | Dumping concentrate too early | Process a batch fully before cleaning up |
| Plant position | Sets haul distance | Plant built far from the pay zone | Rebuild closer as the dig face moves |

Position is the cheapest upgrade you will ever make. A wash plant parked near the active dig face shortens every haul, and as the pit moves, moving the plant with it keeps that advantage. Community reports from the first weeks of Early Access repeatedly mention that relocating the plant is less painful than it sounds — and far less painful than a long haul.

## Hidden Costs: Terrain, Hauling, and Traction

Groundwork Simulator simulates soil and hydraulics in detail, and that detail is where quiet losses hide. Mud, slopes, and rough ground force you to choose routes carefully, and a heavy load behind a truck makes every decision more expensive.

| Hidden cost | How it shows up | What it costs you | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bad traction | Wheels spinning on a wet slope | Fuel and time on every trip | Build a graded access ramp |
| Long haul | Trucks queuing at the plant | Idle excavator time | Move the plant or the stockpile |
| Overloading | Sluggish handling on rough ground | Slower cycle times, stuck loads | Match load size to the route |
| Re-handling | Dirt dug twice | Pure wasted machine hours | Stockpile once, in the right place |
| Dry plant | Waiting on water | Zero recovery while you wait | Lay hoses before the shift starts |

Treat site prep as an investment rather than a chore. Flattening a loading area, benching a slope, or building a short ramp costs one dig cycle and pays back on every load afterward. On a single-map Early Access build, your worksite is your only asset — improving it compounds.

## Solo vs. Co-op: Scaling Without Wasting Time

Online co-op lets you split the job, and splitting it correctly is one of the fastest answers to **Groundwork Simulator how to make money** at scale. The mistake is doubling up on the same task instead of covering the handoffs.

| Task | Solo approach | Two-player approach | Best value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digging | You run the excavator | One dedicated digger | Co-op, if hauling is covered |
| Hauling | You stop digging to drive | Second player shuttles constantly | Co-op, big win |
| Wash plant | You babysit between scoops | One player owns the plant | Co-op, steadier feed |
| Water setup | You build it when the plant stalls | Second player sets it up first | Co-op, zero downtime |
| Site prep | Fits between jobs | A full-time project | Either, plan it deliberately |

A simple division that works: one player never leaves the excavator seat, and the other player owns everything downstream — trucking, water, and plant feed. The excavator is the most expensive machine to leave idle, so protecting its uptime is the whole strategy. If you are playing solo, batch your tasks instead: dig a full stockpile, then haul it all, then run the plant. Switching roles less often is worth more than moving faster.

Because Groundwork Simulator is in Early Access and the developers have said they plan to add more maps and vehicles over roughly three to six months, expect balance and economy tuning to shift. Habits that respect time, water, and haul distance will survive any patch; one-off exploits will not.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the fastest way to make money in Groundwork Simulator?**
Sample first, then commit. A tested pay zone close to a well-fed wash plant beats a large, untested dig every time. Keep haul distances short and never let the plant sit dry.

**Do I need the full wash plant before I can earn anything?**
You need the full chain — dig, wash, concentrate — before material converts into recovery. The story mode tutorial walks you through the sequence, so follow it once before optimizing.

**Is co-op better than solo for profit?**
Usually yes, if you divide roles instead of duplicating them. One player on the excavator and one handling hauling, water, and plant feed keeps the most expensive machine working.

**Will Groundwork Simulator how to make money change during Early Access?**
Likely, since the developers plan new maps and vehicles and are collecting feedback on Discord and Steam. The fundamentals — sampling, water supply, short hauls, and site prep — should stay relevant.
