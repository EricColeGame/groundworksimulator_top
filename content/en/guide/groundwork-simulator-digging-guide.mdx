## Digging Is a Process, Not a Button

Gold doesn't come out of the ground because you own an excavator. It comes out because you dug in the right place, in the right order, and moved the material without wasting a bucket. That is the entire promise of **Groundwork Simulator**: an open world you reshape with heavy machinery while you hunt for paydirt. This **Groundwork Simulator digging guide** breaks the loop into repeatable steps — scouting, testing, digging, hauling, washing — so your session ends with recovered gold instead of a field of half-dug holes.

## Machine Roles: Match the Rig to the Job

The most common early mistake in **Groundwork Simulator** is treating every machine as a digging machine. Each rig has a job it does well and a job that quietly costs you time. Community footage and the game's own promotional material show three machine families working together, so plan your fleet around roles rather than raw size.

| Machine | Primary job | Do this | Avoid this |
|---|---|---|---|
| Excavator | Precision digging and loading | Cut clean faces, load haulers directly, expose deposit layers | Long hauling runs across the map |
| Wheel Loader | Bulk material handling | Clear spoil piles, feed the wash stage, stockpile ore | Fine excavation on uneven ground |
| Bulldozer | Terrain shaping and access | Cut roads, strip overburden, level pads | Digging selective, high-grade material |

A simple rule that holds up in almost any build: **the excavator cuts, the loader moves, the dozer prepares.** If you find yourself driving an excavator three minutes across the map to reach the wash plant, you've already lost the time you saved by digging fast.

Two practical habits follow from that table:

- **Strip before you cut.** Push overburden off the target zone with the dozer first. Digging through sterile top material with an excavator burns the most expensive machine time you have.
- **Build a flat pad.** Loaders and hauling runs behave far better on leveled ground. A five-minute dozer pass near your dig face often saves ten minutes of fighting terrain later.

## The Groundwork Simulator Digging Workflow, Step by Step

This **Groundwork Simulator digging guide** works best as a checklist. Run the same six steps at every site and you'll stop leaving gold in the dirt simply because you lost track of what you were doing.

| Step | Action | Goal | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scout the area | Identify promising ground before touching a bucket | Digging wherever you spawned |
| 2 | Test the deposit | Confirm there's recoverable gold before committing machines | Assuming color in the dirt means a payday |
| 3 | Strip overburden | Expose the layer worth digging | Cutting blind through topsoil |
| 4 | Dig and stage | Keep a steady supply of material at the face | Digging faster than you can haul |
| 5 | Haul to the wash stage | Move material in full loads, one direction | Half-empty buckets and empty return trips |
| 6 | Wash and recover | Process staged material before opening a new pit | Ten open pits, zero gold recovered |

The critical insight is step 4. Digging and hauling should run at the same rate. If your excavator outpaces your hauler, material piles up and you're just relocating dirt twice. If your hauler outpaces your excavator, you're burning fuel and time on empty runs. Match the two, and the whole operation becomes a conveyor belt.

### Keep the Face Clean

A messy dig face is expensive. Collapsed walls, sloped floors, and scattered spoil force you to reposition constantly. Cut a vertical face where the game allows it, keep the floor level enough for a loader to work, and push spoil to one designated side. You'll spend less time repositioning and more time filling buckets — which is the entire point of a **Groundwork Simulator digging guide**.

## How to Test a Deposit Before You Commit

Testing is the step players skip, and it's the one that decides whether a session was profitable. The game's core loop explicitly includes testing deposits before the dig, transport, wash, and recovery stages. Treat a test as cheap information that protects an expensive decision.

| Signal you notice | What it usually means | Best response |
|---|---|---|
| Surface color or visible fragments | Possible gold-bearing ground | Test a small sample before mobilizing |
| Sample comes back barren | Sterile ground at that spot | Move the test grid, don't dig deeper blindly |
| Sample comes back positive | Deposit worth developing | Strip, pad, and set up a proper operation |
| Inconsistent results across a small area | Patchy or narrow deposit | Test wider before investing in infrastructure |
| Consistently strong results | High-value zone | Commit machines, but process material as you go |

A workable testing rhythm for new players:

1. **Test in a grid, not in a line.** Walk a rough square of sample points around the area you like. A single lucky hole tells you almost nothing.
2. **Test before you build.** Don't drag the wash setup to a site you haven't confirmed. Mobility is cheaper than relocation.
3. **Write nothing off after one sample.** Barren results shift a few meters away more often than players expect, per community reports from similar mining sims.

## Hauling and Washing: Where Most Gold Slips Away

Recovery is a chain, and a chain fails at its weakest link. In **Groundwork Simulator**, that link is usually the handoff between digging and washing. Material that sits in a stockpile isn't gold yet, and material that gets washed too fast or too slow is gold you'll never see.

| Stage | What to check | Common mistake | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loading | Bucket is full before you swing | Short swings that move half loads | Fill, then swing — one motion per load |
| Hauling | Route is short and repeatable | Wandering routes, reversing into tight spots | Cut a dedicated road with the dozer |
| Stockpiling | Pile is close to the wash feed | Stockpiling far from processing | Stage material within easy loader range |
| Washing | Feed rate stays steady | Dumping everything at once | Feed at a consistent pace |
| Recovery | You actually collect the output | Walking away before the run finishes | Finish the batch, then re-test the pit |

Two habits that pay off immediately:

- **Process as you dig.** Keep a small batch running through the wash stage while the excavator keeps cutting. You learn faster, and you catch problems before you've moved a mountain of unverified dirt.
- **Don't stockpile what you haven't tested.** A giant pile of sterile material looks like progress and is worth nothing. Test, then pile.

## Co-op Digging and the Mistakes That Sink a Session

Online co-op changes the math. With friends, the bottleneck moves from machine time to coordination — and coordination is where **Groundwork Simulator** groups either thrive or spend twenty minutes asking who took the loader.

### Split Roles Before the First Bucket

| Role | Owner | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Digger | Player 1 | Runs the excavator, keeps the face clean, loads haulers |
| Hauler | Player 2 | Runs the loader or haul truck, keeps a constant cycle |
| Ground crew | Player 3 | Dozer work: roads, pads, overburden stripping |
| Wash operator | Player 4 | Feeds the wash stage, watches recovery, flags bad batches |

If you're running with two players, the digger-hauler pair is the strongest core. Add the dozer only once the road and pad exist.

### Five Mistakes That Waste a Session

- **Digging before testing.** The single biggest time sink in any mining sim, and it compounds in co-op because four people commit at once.
- **Everyone digging, nobody hauling.** Material backs up, the face gets crowded, and progress stalls.
- **No shared plan for the pit.** Two players cutting the same face from opposite sides is how walls collapse.
- **Ignoring the wash stage.** Recovery is the only step that produces gold. Skipping it means you moved dirt for free.
- **Abandoning a good site too early.** If a deposit tested positive, finish it. Chasing a new location resets all your setup work.

Player experience across the genre is consistent on this point: the groups that recover the most gold aren't the ones with the biggest machines, they're the ones with the clearest roles. A **Groundwork Simulator digging guide** can only give you the framework — the discipline has to come from the crew.

## FAQ

**What's the fastest way to start digging in Groundwork Simulator?**
Test first, then dig. Pick a small area, sample several points in a rough grid, and only bring in the excavator once you've confirmed there's gold worth moving. Digging an untested site is the most common way new players waste a session.

**Which machine should I use for digging — the excavator or the loader?**
The excavator does the cutting and loading; the wheel loader moves bulk material and feeds the wash stage. Using a loader as your primary digger works in an emergency, but it's slower and messier on uneven ground.

**Does this Groundwork Simulator digging guide still apply if the game updates?**
The machine roles and the test-dig-haul-wash order are structural, so they should hold across balance changes. Specific dig speeds, deposit yields, and wash rates may shift during Early Access — treat those numbers as moving targets and the workflow as the constant.

**Can I dig faster in co-op?**
Yes, if you split roles instead of doubling up. One player on the excavator and one on hauling will out-produce two players both digging, because the bottleneck moves from the machine to the material flow.

**Is it worth re-testing a pit I already worked?**
Often, yes. Deposits in mining sims tend to be patchy, and a pit that went thin at one edge can still hold material a short distance away. Re-test before you write the site off entirely.

The through-line of this **Groundwork Simulator digging guide** is simple: information before machinery, order before speed, and recovery before the next hole. Dig like that and the gold shows up on its own.
