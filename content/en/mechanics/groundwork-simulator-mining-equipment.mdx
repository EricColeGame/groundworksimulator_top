## Why Your Equipment Chain Decides Everything

Gold doesn't move itself, and in Groundwork Simulator neither does your machinery. Every recovery you bank comes down to how well you pair the right **Groundwork Simulator mining equipment** with the ground in front of you — the excavator that opens the trench, the truck that hauls the pay dirt, and the wash plant that turns a bucket of mud into something worth weighing. Get that chain right and the worksite hums; get it wrong and you spend the day spinning tires on a slope you cut too steep.

This guide breaks down **Groundwork Simulator mining equipment** from pit to plant: what each machine actually does, the order to set things up, how to route water, and how to split the job with a friend in online co-op. It's written for players in the current Early Access build, which ships with one playable map, a roster of heavy machinery, and a story mode that walks you through the core mechanics. Since the developers plan to expand with more maps and vehicles, treat this as a framework rather than a fixed list — the logic of digging, hauling, and washing should hold up as the roster grows.

## What Counts as Mining Equipment in Groundwork Simulator

The game's mining loop isn't one machine doing everything. It's a sequence of tools, each one feeding the next. The [official Groundwork Simulator page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5306540/Groundwork_Simulator/) frames it as reshaping a landscape and uncovering gold, with equipment turning a bucket of dirt into a recovery. Here's how the roles shake out in practice.

| Equipment | Primary job | Where it sits in the chain | Player note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Excavator | Digging, trenching, cutting hillsides, loading | Front of the chain | Your highest-skill machine; bucket angle and hydraulic power matter |
| Haul truck / transport | Moving raw material from pit to plant | Middle of the chain | Bottleneck if you dig faster than you haul |
| Truck and trailer | Relocating machinery between worksites | Site-to-site logistics | Loading alignment and tight turns are the real challenge |

The takeaway: **Groundwork Simulator mining equipment** is a system, not a shopping list. Buying or deploying more iron doesn't help if the material can't flow from bucket to sluice.

## The Dig-to-Gold Workflow, Step by Step

Most inefficient worksites fail at the same point — players start digging before they've tested the ground or plumbed the plant. Community reports from early players consistently flag this as the most expensive habit. Try this order instead.

| Step | Action | Equipment involved | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scout the land for promising ground | On foot / survey | You can't wash gold that isn't there |
| 2 | Drill and test for deposits | Drill / test setup | Confirms where to commit your pit |
| 3 | Position the wash plant and sluice boxes | Wash plant | Sets your haul distance before digging starts |
| 4 | Run water lines to the plant | Pumps and hoses | A dry plant is a dead plant |
| 5 | Open the pit and stockpile material | Excavator | Keeps a buffer so the plant never starves |
| 6 | Haul material to the plant | Haul truck | The bridge between digging and washing |
| 7 | Process concentrate | Shaking table | Converts effort into recovery |
| 8 | Improve the ground you damaged | Excavator | Roads and ramps you cut now save hours later |

Step 8 is the one people skip. Every bucket changes the landscape, and the ground you leave behind becomes the foundation for your next job. A ramp you graded on purpose beats a slope you created by accident every single time.

### Reading the Terrain Before You Cut

Mud, slopes, and rough ground make every route a decision. Before you commit an excavator to a hillside, ask three questions: can a loaded truck climb out of here, can I turn around at the bottom, and where does water pool after rain or after I break a hose? If the answer to any of those is "no," cut the access first. Traction is a mechanic, not a decoration — a heavy load on soft ground will stop you cold.

## Matching Groundwork Simulator Mining Equipment to the Job

Not every machine belongs on every site. The table below maps common situations to sensible equipment choices.

| Situation | Weak answer | Strong answer |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit confirmed, plant not built | Dig immediately | Build the plant and water line first |
| Long haul between pit and plant | One overloaded truck run | Shorten the haul, or stage a stockpile mid-route |
| Steep hillside cut | Straight down the face | Bench it in steps, then ramp out |
| Plant starving for feed | Dig harder | Pre-stockpile a buffer with the excavator |
| Moving to a new worksite | Drive everything | Load machinery onto the trailer and haul it |
| Muddy access road | More throttle | Grade the ground, then drive |

That last row is the most common trap. Sometimes the next job is improving the ground beneath your wheels before you can get back to digging. It feels like a detour; it's usually the fastest path.

## Water, Sluice Boxes, and the Wash Plant

The wash plant is where **Groundwork Simulator mining equipment** stops being about dirt and starts being about plumbing. Water comes from pumps and hoses, and the plant only performs when supply keeps pace with the feed you're dumping in.

| Setup stage | What to do | Failure symptom if skipped |
|---|---|---|
| Position the plant | Place it near the pit but above the waterline | Long hauls or a flooded pad |
| Lay out pumps | Set pumps at the source, not the plant | Weak pressure at the sluice |
| Route hoses | Keep runs short and off vehicle paths | Crushed lines, constant repairs |
| Test flow | Run water before the first bucket | Dry sluice, wasted material |
| Scale up | Add capacity as the pit grows | Plant becomes the bottleneck |

As your worksite expands, adapt the setup rather than rebuilding it. Adding a pump or rerouting a hose is cheap; relocating a plant mid-operation is not. Plan hose routes the way you plan roads — out of the way of anything with wheels.

## Splitting Groundwork Simulator Mining Equipment in Co-op

Online co-op changes the math. Two players can divide the work so no machine sits idle, and the classic split is digger versus driver.

| Role | Machine | Responsibilities | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operator | Excavator | Cut, stockpile, load trucks, maintain ramps | Don't out-dig the haul; keep the ramp clean |
| Hauler | Truck and trailer | Move material, relocate machinery, keep the route clear | Overloading on slopes kills momentum |
| Plant hand | Pumps, hoses, wash plant | Maintain water flow, monitor feed rate | Silent failures — check the sluice regularly |
| Switcher | Any | Fill gaps, grade roads, handle site moves | Communicate; don't duplicate work |

Community reports suggest the smoothest co-op sites have one player who owns the pit and one who owns the plant, with transport shared. If you both dig, you both end up waiting on a truck. If you both haul, the pit stalls. Coordination beats raw machine count.

## FAQ

**How much does Groundwork Simulator mining equipment cost to get started?**
The game itself is the only purchase — there's no separate equipment DLC mentioned on the store page. It launched at $24.99 with a 20% introductory discount to $19.99, and the developers have said they don't plan to change the base price when it leaves Early Access.

**Do I need to unlock Groundwork Simulator mining equipment, or is it all available?**

**What's the most common mistake with Groundwork Simulator mining equipment?**
Digging before testing. Players open a pit, haul material to a plant, and only then discover the ground wasn't gold-rich. Drill and test first, position the plant second, and dig third.

**Can I share Groundwork Simulator mining equipment with friends?**
Yes — online co-op lets you divide the work, with one player running the excavator while another handles transport, or both keeping material moving between the digging area and the wash plant. Coordinate roles so no machine sits idle.
