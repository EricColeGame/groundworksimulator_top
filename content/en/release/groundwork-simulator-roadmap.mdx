## Early Access Timeline: What Three to Six Months Really Means

The developers expect Groundwork Simulator to stay in Early Access for roughly three to six months, and they explicitly note that the window can shift based on development progress and community feedback. That's a normal caveat, but it's worth planning around rather than treating as a promise.

If the estimate holds, a full 1.0 release would land somewhere between late January and late April 2027. That's arithmetic, not an announcement — the studio has not committed to a date, and delays in simulation-heavy games are common because physics and terrain systems are expensive to tune.

## Planned Content: Maps, Vehicles, and Features on the Horizon

The two headline items on the **Groundwork Simulator roadmap** are more maps and more vehicles. At launch the game ships with one playable map, a range of heavy machinery, and a story mode that teaches the core loop through a guided tutorial. Everything beyond that is expansion.

What's already in the build is a substantial foundation, and it's worth understanding it before speculating about what's coming:

- **Terrain deformation** — every bucket changes the landscape permanently, creating new routes, slopes, and obstacles
- **Excavation physics** — simulated hydraulic feedback means the bucket resists, loads up, and responds to your angle and available power
- **Gold processing chain** — drill and test for deposits, excavate, run material through sluice boxes, then process concentrate on a shaking table
- **Water system** — pumps and hoses must be positioned and routed to keep the wash plant supplied
- **Transport** — load machinery onto a trailer and haul it by truck across tight turns, slopes, and soft ground
- **Traction and terrain** — mud and grades force route decisions, and sometimes you have to fix the ground before you can dig

## How Community Feedback Shapes the Groundwork Simulator Roadmap

This is the part most players underestimate. The developers have named two official channels for feedback: Steam Community discussions and the official Discord server. Both are used for bug reports, feature suggestions, and general discussion, and the studio says player input will help set development priorities.

| Channel | Best Used For | Tips for Getting Noticed |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Community discussions | Bug reports, feature requests, general feedback | Search before posting; add your system specs and a short clip |
| Official Discord server | Real-time discussion, quick bug confirmation | Use the designated bug and suggestion channels |
| Development updates | Progress and change tracking | Read before assuming something was ignored |

A few actionable habits that make feedback more useful:

1. **Report one issue per post.** Bundling five bugs into one thread usually means four get missed.
2. **Include your hardware.** The game is CPU-heavy by design, so performance reports without specs are hard to act on.
3. **Clip it.** A 15-second video of a bucket clipping through terrain is worth a paragraph of description.
4. **Separate bugs from wishes.** "The excavator sinks through the road" and "I want a bigger excavator" belong in different places.
5. **Be patient with the pace.** A small team shipping physics updates can't respond to every thread within a day.
