## What You Can Actually Do on Day One

The launch build is not a thin vertical slice. According to the developer's own description of the current state, it includes most of the features planned for the full release. Here's what that translates to in practice:

- **Terrain deformation that sticks.** Every bucket removes material permanently. Trenches, cut hillsides, and stockpiles you create become the terrain you drive on later, which means bad planning has real consequences.
- **Hydraulic digging physics.** The excavation engine simulates resistance as the bucket loads up, so angle and available hydraulic power both matter.
- **A full gold-processing chain.** Drill and test for deposits, dig the promising ground, run material through sluice boxes, then finish the concentrate on a shaking table.
- **Water logistics.** Pumps and hoses have to be routed across your site to keep the wash plant fed as the operation expands.
- **Machine transport.** Load equipment onto a trailer, haul it by truck, and wrestle with tight turns, slopes, and soft ground under load.
- **Online co-op.** Split roles — one player on the excavator, another on hauling — or run the whole site solo.

## Day-One Tips for New Players

Because terrain changes are permanent, a few habits early on save hours later. These are practical, not theoretical — they follow directly from how the excavation and water systems are described.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Finish the story tutorial first | It teaches the core loop before you commit to a site layout |
| 2 | Test for gold before digging | Drilling and testing saves you from moving dirt with no payoff |
| 3 | Plan your haul routes early | Every scoop reshapes the ground you'll drive on next |
| 4 | Route water before expanding | The wash plant is useless without a steady supply |
| 5 | Improve ground under wheels | Mud and slopes cost more time than a short detour |
| 6 | Bring a co-op partner for hauling | Splitting excavator and transport duty roughly doubles throughput |

If you're playing solo, resist the urge to expand the site quickly. A compact operation with a short dig-to-wash distance will outperform a sprawling one where half your time goes into driving.
