### Terrain That Remembers

The excavation engine is the headline. Trenches, hillside cuts, and stockpiles all persist, so the ramp you carve on day one is the ramp you drive on day ten — or the slope that slides your loaded truck sideways. Because material builds up inside the bucket and hydraulic power is finite, you have to pick angles deliberately. Community reports from the first day of Early Access focus heavily on this: the terrain is the antagonist, and grading a road is often a smarter first move than chasing a gold signal.

### From Dirt to Gold

The progression chain runs from prospecting to processing. You explore for gold-rich ground, drill and test deposits, then set up a wash plant. Excavated material goes through sluice boxes, and the concentrate gets finished on a shaking table. Water is the quiet bottleneck — pumps and hoses have to be positioned and routed across a site that keeps changing shape as you dig it.

## Practical Tips for Your First Hours

The learning curve is mostly about patience, not reflexes. These habits will save you a rebuild.

| Step | Action | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish story mode before free play | Teaches hydraulic limits and bucket control with guardrails |
| 2 | Drill test holes before mobilizing the plant | Avoids hauling equipment to barren ground |
| 3 | Grade a flat pad early | Traction and stability improve everywhere downstream |
| 4 | Route water before you need it | Relocating pumps mid-operation wastes a full cycle |
| 5 | Split roles in co-op | One player digs while the other keeps material moving |

Additional pointers from early player experience:

- **Dig with the slope, not against it.** Approaching a cut from the downhill side reduces the material you have to re-handle.
- **Build stockpiles near the wash plant, not at the hole.** Haul distance is the hidden cost in every operation.
- **Improve the ground under your wheels before chasing a load.** Mud and steep grades punish a heavy trailer far more than an empty one.
- **Save before major earthworks.** Undoing a badly placed cut is slower than reloading.
